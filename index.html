Common Questions

Answers loan officers actually ask.

Direct answers to the questions loan officers and brokers research before partnering with a contract processor.

What is third-party mortgage processing?

Third-party mortgage processing is when a loan officer or broker outsources the processing of a mortgage file to an independent, licensed processor outside their own company. The third-party processor manages the file from application to clear-to-close — collecting documentation, structuring the file, submitting to underwriting, clearing conditions, and coordinating with realtors and title — while the loan officer focuses on origination.

What is a third party mortgage processor?

A third party mortgage processor is an independent, licensed loan processor who handles loan files for outside loan officers and mortgage brokers on a per-file or contract basis. Unlike an in-house processor (a payroll employee), a third party processor operates under their own NMLS license and serves multiple loan officer clients. Star Processing is a third party mortgage processor based in Charlotte, NC — Licensed Loan Officer in NC, Licensed Mortgage Broker in SC, NMLS #2636616.

What does a contract loan processor do?

A contract loan processor — also called a third-party mortgage processor — manages a mortgage file from application to clear-to-close on behalf of a loan officer or broker. The processor collects documentation, structures the file, submits it to underwriting, clears conditions, coordinates with realtors and title companies, and communicates with the borrower — without being a payroll employee.

Should I hire a contract loan processor or an in-house processor?

A contract processor is best when volume is variable, when you want to scale without payroll overhead, or when you need overflow during busy seasons. An in-house processor only makes financial sense at consistently high volume — typically 8–12 closed files per month per processor. Many loan officers run a hybrid: in-house for steady volume, contract for overflow.

How long does mortgage loan processing take?

Most conventional purchase loans close in 21–30 days from contract. Refinances run 30–45 days. Non-QM, DSCR, and jumbo loans take longer due to documentation complexity. Skilled processors accelerate timelines through pre-underwriting file structuring, proactive condition clearing, and direct communication with realtors and title.

Can a contract loan processor handle non-QM and DSCR loans?

Yes — but experience matters. Non-QM and DSCR loans require careful file structuring because documentation requirements differ from agency loans. An experienced processor familiar with alternative-doc products and investment property files will handle these efficiently. Star Processing handles non-QM, DSCR, and investment property files at the standard $995 per-file fee.

What are NMLS call reports?

NMLS (Nationwide Multistate Licensing System) call reports are quarterly and annual filings that licensed mortgage entities must submit through the NMLS Resource Center. Quarterly call reports are due 45 days after quarter-end; annual financial reports 90 days after year-end. Missing or inaccurate filings can result in fines or license suspension.

Do contract loan processors handle FHA, VA, and condo loans?

Yes. Most experienced contract processors handle the full range of government-backed loans (FHA, VA) alongside Conventional, Jumbo, Non-QM, DSCR, investment property loans, HELOCs, and home equity lines. Government loans require additional documentation like a Certificate of Eligibility (VA) or appraisal compliance (FHA), which an experienced processor manages routinely.

What is condition management in mortgage processing?

Condition management is the process of clearing the documentation and verification requirements an underwriter places on a loan after initial review. Conditions might include updated pay stubs, letters of explanation, gift letters, verification of employment, or appraisal corrections. A processor pursues conditions actively — gathering documents from borrowers, communicating with verifiers, and returning items to underwriting.

What is pre-underwriting file structuring?

Pre-underwriting file structuring is the upfront review of a borrower's income, assets, and credit before submission to underwriting. It identifies potential issues early — inconsistencies, missing documentation, qualifying gaps — so files reach underwriting clean and complete. The result is fewer conditions, faster approvals, and no surprises at closing.

Can a contract loan processor help with NMLS compliance?

Some contract processors offer compliance support beyond loan files — including help with quarterly and annual NMLS call reports, state regulatory reporting, and documentation organization for NMLS and state audits. Especially valuable for independent loan officers and small brokerages without dedicated compliance staff. Star Processing offers this as part of its services.

What is overflow mortgage processing support?

Overflow processing is contract-based surge support when an in-house processing team is at capacity. The contract processor handles additional volume on a per-file or short-term basis, allowing the loan officer to keep accepting business without missing closings or hiring permanent staff. Commonly used during seasonal volume spikes or rate-driven refinance booms.

Should new mortgage brokers use a contract loan processor?

Yes. New brokers benefit because they get experienced, detail-driven file management without committing to a full-time salary. The broker focuses on origination, building referral relationships, and learning the business — while an experienced processor handles compliance, condition clearing, and back-office discipline.

Do contract loan processors work with realtors and title companies?

A good contract loan processor communicates directly with realtors and title companies on the loan officer's behalf. Direct coordination keeps timelines aligned, prevents miscommunication, and protects the loan officer's referral relationships. Always confirm this in advance — some processors only work file-internally and leave external communication to the loan officer.

How can a loan officer scale their business without hiring?

Loan officers scale without hiring by using contract loan processors, leveraging tech for borrower communication and document collection, building strong referral pipelines so volume comes inbound, and delegating compliance and back-office work to specialized contractors. The contract model converts a fixed payroll cost into a variable per-file cost that scales with revenue.

What questions should I ask before hiring a contract loan processor?

Ask about: years of experience and loan types they've handled; whether they communicate directly with realtors, title, and borrowers; their pricing model (per-file, contract, or hybrid); turnaround time on conditions; experience with non-QM and government loans; whether they offer compliance and NMLS support; and references from current loan officer partners.

Is same-day mortgage loan processing possible?

Same-day mortgage closing is generally not possible because federal TRID/TILA-RESPA rules require minimum disclosure waiting periods (typically 3 business days). However, same-day file structuring is — a responsive contract processor begins work within hours, completes pre-underwriting review the same day, and submits to underwriting once documents are gathered. Star Processing typically responds to new files within 24 hours.
